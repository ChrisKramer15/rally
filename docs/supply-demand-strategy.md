# Supply & Demand Zone Trading — Full Summary

A complete walkthrough of a supply/demand zone strategy the creator has traded for ~5 years. It works across any market (stocks, forex, futures) and any timeframe, for both day traders and swing traders.

## What a zone actually is (and isn't)

- **Not** just every high and low of price. That's basically support/resistance dressed up in different terms.
- A true zone requires two things:
  1. **Basing** — a tight consolidation where buyers/sellers are roughly balanced (equilibrium).
  2. **A strong, explosive move away** — the imbalance created when one side exhausts the other.
- Don't cut through candles when drawing zones. That's support/resistance.
- Support/resistance looks for *multiple* touches; supply/demand trades the **first** touch of a **fresh** zone.
- Train your eye to spot the strong move first, then find the basing. Ignore slow, stair-stepping "hesitation" moves.

## The logic behind why it works

- Zones aren't created intentionally, but banks and institutions leave **footprints** on the chart.
- Only large players (banks, institutions) can create explosive imbalance candles — retail can't move a major market like that.
- Core idea: institutions left **unfilled orders** at the origin of a big move. When price returns, you enter alongside them to fill the leftover orders.
- You are **not** predicting the first explosive move — you're reacting to the pullback into the zone.

## Types of zones

- **Peaks & Valleys (VP):** reversal patterns, mostly found at tops/bottoms.
  - Demand: **Drop-Base-Rally**
  - Supply: **Rally-Base-Drop**
- **Continuation Patterns (CP):** pauses mid-trend (the creator's favorite). Often single-candle basing.
  - Demand: **Rally-Base-Rally**
  - Supply: **Drop-Base-Drop**

## Zone structure & how to draw it

- Two lines:
  - **Proximal line** — closest to current price.
  - **Distal line** — furthest from current price.
- **Basing quality:** best is tight, 1–4 candles. Messy 10+ candle basing signals a weak imbalance.
- **Move away quality:** Poor → Decent → A+. A+ = explosive, large-body candles with little/no wick. Target A+.
- **Drawing (demand):** distal at the lowest wick of the basing candles, proximal at the top of the basing body/wick. Capture the whole basing (don't cut candles). Supply is the inverse.
- Use TradingView's **magnet tool** to snap lines precisely to wicks/highs/lows.
- **Zone size tradeoff:**
  - Small zone → best risk/reward, but easily missed or stopped out.
  - Large zone → fills easily, but poor R:R.
  - Medium is the creator's preferred balance.

## Entries

- **Preferred (~98% of the time):** pending **limit order** just before the proximal line. Set entry, stop, and target, then let it play out.
- **Market order:** only if you're late to a zone. Not recommended — can push your stop wider and invites FOMO.
- Never force a trade. Missing one is fine.

## Stop loss

- Place it a little **below** the zone (demand) — never exactly on the distal line, or spread can stop you out.
- Tighter stop → better R:R but more likely to be hit by normal fluctuations.
- Wider stop → more breathing room but lower R:R.
- Backtest to find what fits you.
- **Never move a stop wider** after entry. Ever.

## Take profit (many valid methods)

- **Fixed R:R** (creator's preference) — e.g., 2:1, 3:1, 5:1.
- **Opposing zones** — target the nearest opposing supply/demand.
- **Opposing structure** — prior highs/lows.
- **No TP + trailing stop** — trail behind fresh zones/structure to ride bigger moves.
- **Hybrid** — take partial profit at a fixed R:R and let a runner go.

## Trade management

- There is **no universal rule** for moving stops. Be skeptical of anyone speaking in absolutes.
- Options:
  - Don't touch it — let the trade fully play out.
  - Move to **break-even** after a set R:R (can stop you out too early).
  - Move to **half-risk** after a set R:R (creator's favorite — cuts risk while leaving room).
  - Trail with price/structure.

## Timeframes & multiple timeframe analysis

- Map zones on a **higher timeframe (HTF)**, wait for price to reach them, then drop to the **entry timeframe (ETF)** for a fresh zone in the same direction.
- Price is **fractal** — the exact same method applies on any timeframe.
- Common sequences:
  - Day trading: 1m/15m, 5m/1h, 15m/4h
  - Swing trading: 1h/daily, 4h/weekly
- Higher timeframe zones dominate lower ones (e.g., weekly demand beats a 1h supply).
- Recommends **swing trading for beginners** — slower, less stress, works alongside a 9–5. Day trading needs fast decisions and constant monitoring (more advanced).

## Odds boosters (optional confluence)

- **Qualifying a zone:** it should break an opposing zone OR remove a trend line (both is best). Zone must be **fresh** — untested zones lose reliability.
- **Confirmation entry:** wait for a lower-timeframe zone or trendline break inside the HTF zone before entering.
- **Set & forget:** trade fresh zones aligned with the HTF trend, no confirmation needed.
- **Strength of the move away:** explosive "marubozu" candles (large body, little/no wick).
- **Liquidity traps:** a support/resistance level sitting just *before* your zone traps retail traders. Their stop-losses feed your entry — you take the opposite side of the losing crowd.

## Trading psychology

- Losses are part of a probabilistic game — a 4–8 trade losing streak is normal.
- Know your break-even win rate (e.g., 2:1 R:R only needs ~33% wins to break even).
- Use a profit simulator (creator mentions winrate.io) or AI to learn your **max and average drawdowns** so streaks don't shock you.
- Avoid **revenge trading** and **strategy hopping**. Stick to a backtested plan.
- **Realistic expectations:** 20–30% per year is excellent. Beating the market's 8–10% already puts you ahead. Ignore gurus flashing huge profits — they're easy to fake.
- Learn to *trade* first; money follows. Keep a high-paying job for income and to remove stress. Don't expect to quit your 9–5. If it sounds too good to be true, it is.
